# Domain glossary

## Family adventure

A short, complete game that children aged roughly 8–12 can understand and finish independently, while offering optional tactical depth that remains enjoyable for adults.

## Monster

An original collectible creature created for this game. A monster has a recognizable identity and can join the player’s team and participate in battles.

## Team

The small set of caught monsters available for the player to choose from during the adventure and its battles.

## Story starter

The player’s first monster, which joins automatically through the opening story. It enables immediate exploration and battle instruction before later monsters are earned through catching challenges.

## Catching challenge

The activity through which a monster joins the player’s team. Its exact form is a design decision; a separate minigame is optional rather than assumed.

## Discover–catch–test

The adventure’s repeating middle rhythm: explore a short area and discover a monster, complete its catching challenge, then encounter a nearby battle where that monster provides a readable advantage.

## Readable tactic

A meaningful choice whose relevant clues and consequences are visible enough for a child to reason about, while still allowing an adult to optimize.

## Five-affinity pentagram

The battle matchup graph in which each of five affinities has an advantage over two others, is disadvantaged against the remaining two, and is equal against itself. Every opposing affinity therefore has two valid counters, allowing richer team choices than a single prescribed answer. Basic attacks use three clear damage bands: approximately 150% when advantaged, 100% against the same affinity, and 50% when disadvantaged.

## Single affinity

Each monster belongs to exactly one affinity for both attacking and defending. A monster’s matchup is therefore immediately readable without combining or resolving multiple elemental rules.

## Simple numeric health

Monsters use visible numerical health and fixed-effect actions without attack, defense, speed, level, or experience statistics. Basic attacks have consistent base damage, while monsters may have small maximum-health differences. Affinity modifies action damage directly, preserving familiar JRPG feedback without a broader stat system.

## Talent

A monster’s single distinctive battle action alongside its reliable basic attack. A talent has a simple effect and can be used once per battle, giving the monster personality and creating one clear timing decision without requiring a large move list or resource meter. Child-created talent ideas are mapped onto a small set of balanced effect templates, preserving their names and flavor without introducing bespoke rules for every monster.

## Counter-switch

A switch that consumes the player’s turn while the opponent’s announced attack lands on the incoming monster. Choosing an advantageous affinity reduces that incoming damage, so switching carries risk but immediately rewards reading the matchup correctly.

## Readable opponent script

A short authored sequence of computer actions and monster appearances. Before every turn, the opponent’s next attack or talent is announced so the player can learn the pattern, evaluate a counter-switch, and plan without hidden optimal behavior. The player’s chosen action resolves first; a knockout cancels the opponent’s announced action. Opponents do not continuously counter-switch.

## Affinity notebook

An in-game record that permanently stores elemental relationships after the player observes them in combat. The battle screen shows affinity identities but does not directly label choices as strong or weak, leaving the player to remember or consult discovered knowledge. The notebook can be opened freely during battle without consuming a turn.

## Immediate retry

A failure response that restarts only the short battle or catching challenge, preserves all earlier progress, and may provide a clearer hint after repeated attempts.

## Encounter recovery

Automatic full restoration of team health and once-per-battle talents after every victory. Each encounter is therefore a self-contained tactical challenge rather than part of an attrition or healing-resource system.

## Team knockout

A monster at zero health becomes unavailable for the remainder of the current encounter, and the player chooses another available teammate. The encounter is lost and immediately retried only when the entire team is knocked out.

## Child-owned content

Creative work whose recognizable identity comes from the children, even when an adult simplifies, digitizes, balances, or integrates it.

## Supporting asset

Purchased art or audio used to fill gaps and complement child-owned content without defining the game’s identity.

## Family workshop

A collaborative 30–60-minute family session, optimized for two children and two adults but tolerant of absences or a few guests. Everyone contributes independently before a simultaneous reveal and equal private vote; each child protects one feasible ingredient before the group combines ideas into one visible, group-owned creative outcome.

## Workshop decision contract

The opening promise for a family workshop: it identifies the exact game contribution being created, the choices children control, the fixed constraints, and the transformations the adult may make later. Accepted choices must remain recognizable in the finished game; infeasible choices are screened out before children vote rather than silently replaced afterward.

## Guided small world

A compact, continuous adventure space with a clear goal and short connected areas. It offers limited freedom to choose where to explore next without allowing navigation or optional content to undermine the 30-minute pacing.

## Thirty-minute path

The smooth critical route through the complete adventure, intentionally targeting approximately 30 minutes without a countdown. A younger child’s first completion may take approximately 40–45 minutes once reading, exploration, and immediate retries are included.

## Affinity discovery tutorial

A safe scripted opening exchange that reveals and records one advantageous relationship and one disadvantageous relationship before the player makes a genuine battle choice. It teaches both combat feedback and use of the affinity notebook.

## Whole-team finale

A staged final encounter that asks the player to recognize several previously taught matchup clues and switch among the monsters earned during the adventure.

## Celebration showcase

The closing sequence after the finale: resolve the immediate story, show the completed team together, visibly credit the children’s creations, and then offer replay.
